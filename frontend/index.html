A True Story // Apollo 14 · February 1971 · Cone Crater

Thirty Meters Decided the Mission.

Alan Shepard and Edgar Mitchell turned back roughly thirty meters from Cone Crater when the terrain and their oxygen limits made the final push too costly. NeuroNexus asks the same question before an EVA: what does the map hide at walking scale?

Imagery & Context: LROC (NASA/GSFC/Arizona State University) · Apollo 14 AS14-64-9089 (NASA). Open source image

Our Approach

Orbital maps blur the short, steep pitches a person would actually walk on. NeuroNexus stacks NASA's , and terrain, checks it against the tilt Curiosity and Perseverance really felt, and turns it into an plan with a .

Every value carries its source. Missing data stays missing. Planning limits are labeled as planning limits, never as safety limits.

    Where NASA Data Becomes a Walking Plan.

    • 01

      Planning Limits Are Not Safety Limits

      Every status reads Within Planning Limits, Review Required or Exceeds Planning Limit. No route or ground receives a safety verdict.

    • 02

      No Hallucinated Values

      Where NASA data does not reach, the console says n/a or Unavailable. A gap is shown as a gap, never filled with a guess.

    • 03

      AI May Explain, Never Invent

      A deterministic engine owns every route and number. answers through and labels each answer as AI interpretation.
