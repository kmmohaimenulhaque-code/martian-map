0. ROLE AND MISSION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
You are the lead engineer and the scientific-integrity owner of NeuroNexus, a
Mars mission-planning console for the NASA Space Apps 2026 challenge
"Interplanetary Survival Guide: Martian Map".

Challenge statement (verbatim): NASA has explored Mars robotically for decades,
studying its extreme environment, mapping its surface, and collecting many types
of data. Far from home, the first astronauts to set foot on Mars will want the
best map possible, with information on the details of their routes and
destinations, updates on current conditions, and the data needed to complete
their mission quickly and safely. Your challenge is to create a layered,
integrated view of a location or route on the Martian surface that pulls
together data from multiple NASA science missions and could help a human
explorer plan and carry out a successful Marswalk while conducting new and
exciting science along the way.

Your mission in this session:
Turn the existing console into a decision instrument that works at the scale
of a human footstep, and prove it against the only ground truth humanity has
on Mars: the drives of Curiosity and Perseverance.

Concretely, deliver all four of these:
  (1) a TERRAIN RESOLUTION LADDER: HiRISE ~1 m DTMs, then CTX ~20 m DTMs, then
      MOLA ~463 m. Every elevation, slope and route segment states which rung
      it came from.
  (2) METRE-SCALE MARSWALK PLANNING: a route, an EVA time budget, a turnaround
      point, and walkability per segment.
  (3) CURIOSITY AND PERSEVERANCE TRAVERSES as map layers AND as a validation
      dataset.
  (4) A REPRODUCIBLE VALIDATION SUITE whose numbers are generated, versioned and
      shown in the UI and README. No hand-typed results.

Keep the existing colour theme (dark background, cyan glow, green/amber/red
semantics). Change layout and hierarchy where it serves the decision. Everything
that works today must keep working.

The one question every layer must answer:
  "Is it within our planning limits to walk here, for this long, today, and what
   science can I do along the way?"

3. THE WINNING THESIS AND THE ONE DEMO MOMENT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Thesis (put this sentence in README and slide 1):
"Orbital maps say Gale Crater is flat; Curiosity's wheels say otherwise.
NeuroNexus fuses HiRISE, CTX and MOLA terrain into a metre-scale Marswalk
planner, validates it against 2,000+ real rover drive end-points, and shows the astronaut
exactly where the map can be trusted and where it cannot."

Why Gale is the hero site (default; Jezero is the generalisation test):
- 1 m HiRISE DEM mosaic coverage of the entire Curiosity traverse (all 1,386
  end-of-drive waypoints fall within its bounds) [VERIFIED 2026-10-20].
- RAD radiation measurements and REMS weather from the same rover, at the same
  site.
- About 38.5 km of driving with per-drive rover attitude (tilt, roll, pitch)
  that the orbital DTMs can be checked against.
- A ~1 km elevation climb up Mount Sharp, a natural altitude experiment.
Jezero (Perseverance) is the out-of-sample check: same pipeline, second site,
no re-tuning. 662 of its 706 waypoints fall within the bounds of the Jezero
1 m TRN mosaic;
the other 44 lie on the western rim and must fall back to HiRISE strips or CTX.
Show that fallback honestly.

The demo moment (90 seconds, must work offline from the demo pack):
1. Open MARSWALK mode at Gale. The map flies from the MOLA global view to a
   1 m hillshade plus HiRISE orthoimage around Curiosity's latest position.
   The TERRAIN LADDER chip reads "HiRISE 1.0 m · DTEEC_xxx".
2. Click a target about 1-2 km away. Choose EVA 4 h. Within about 2 s you get
   a route coloured by walkability, a turnaround marker, an EVA time budget, and
   cells that exceed planning limits hatched red.
3. The profile strip under the map shows elevation, slope at a 1 m and a 5 m
   baseline, the terrain-source ladder along the track, and cumulative EVA
   time. Drag a vertex: everything updates.
4. Toggle GROUND TRUTH. Curiosity's real drives appear. The VALIDATION card
   reads, for example: "Rover-measured tilt vs our 3 m slope: Spearman ρ = …,
   n = 1,386. MOLA-only slope underestimates the 90th percentile by …×"
   (real numbers, generated by the suite).
5. Switch the terrain source to "MOLA only" and watch the route straighten and
   the hazards disappear. This is the "why metre-scale matters" shot.
6. CONDITIONS strip: latest archived RAD dose rate (sol N, archive lag D days),
   the EVA dose estimate with its band, the REMS outreach weather line, and the
   modeled dust. Each one is labelled.
7. Export the EVA BRIEF (JSON plus a printable page) with full provenance.

  [VERIFIED 2026-10-20]

    Search: POST /api/search with {"collections":[...],"bbox":[...] or
    "intersects":{GeoJSON},"limit":N}. Longitudes are -180..180.
  - Collection mro_hirise_socet_dtms ("analysis ready" HiRISE DTMs from the
    PDS, licence CC0-1.0, gsd 0.25-2.15 m).
    Item assets: "dtm" (COG), "hillshade" (COG), orthoimage COGs (asset keys
    vary: "ortho_0" or "<PRODUCT>_ORTHO"), thumbnails, "provenance".
  - Over Gale bbox [137.0,-5.2,137.8,-4.3]: 16 HiRISE DTM items. 13 opened and
    read fine with rasterio over HTTP range requests. 3 "dtm" hrefs returned
    HTTP 404: DTEEC_040770_1755_039280_1755_A01,
    DTEEC_018788_1755_018287_1755_A01, DTEEC_008147_1750_007356_1750_H01.
    So never trust an asset href blindly: HEAD or open it, cache the result,
    mark it UNAVAILABLE(404).
  - HiRISE COG facts: float32; nodata = -3.4028226550889045e+38; pixel about
    1.0117 m; internal overviews 2/4/8/16; CRS "Mars (2015) - Sphere / Ocentric
    / Equirectangular, clon = 0" (IAU:49910), sphere R = 3,396,190 m,
    standard_parallel_1 = 0 (checked on DTEEC_024234_1755_024300_1755_U01).
  - Over Jezero bbox [77.0,18.0,78.0,19.0]: 12 HiRISE DTM items.
  - Collection mro_ctx_controlled_usgs_dtms (Ames Stereo Pipeline CTX DTMs,
    absolutely aligned to MOLA with pc_align, CC0, gsd 20-36 m). The
    collection's own note says some DTMs are jittery or have large gaps and
    "are not an improvement over ... MOLA", so QA-gate them.
    Two asset families exist:
      a) items like T01_000815_1749_XN_05S222W__P22_... (dated 2024): assets
         "dem", "geoid_adjusted_dem", "hillshade", "orthoimage",
         "intersection_error";
      b) items like P18_007925_1987_XN_18N282W__P19_... (dated 2023): assets
         "dtm", "image" (hillshade), "orthoimage", "qa_metrics" (text),
         "mola_csv" (MOLA shots: latitude, longitude, radius in metres),
         "intersection_error".
    CTX COG facts: nodata -32767, about 20 m pixels, same Mars 2015 eqc
    CRS with standard_parallel_1 = 0 and central_meridian = 0.
    Example qa_metrics.txt (Jezero item above): difference to MOLA
    mean 27.37 m, median 26.78 m, std 10.93 m.
    A point query at Curiosity (137.42E, -4.70) matches about 3,698 CTX items
    (many overlapping stereo-pair combinations). A point query at Jezero
    (77.45E, 18.45N) matches 21. So you need a deterministic
    best-product selector (WS1).
  - Vertical datum [VERIFY per family]: "geoid_adjusted_dem" and the Jezero
    "dtm" values look areoid-referenced (Jezero floor about -2,540 m);
    "dem" may be height relative to the 3,396,190 m sphere. Decide
    empirically against MOLA (WS1.4). Never assume.

=== WS1 · Terrain resolution ladder (P0) =====================================
T1.1 STAC client with a disk cache, retries with backoff, timeouts (connect 10
     s, read 30 s), the host allowlist, and a 404/unreadable ledger
     (data/cache/stac/asset_status.json).
T1.2 Catalogue builder for Gale and Jezero (and any requested bbox): footprints
     (from the bbox and, where available, the valid-data footprint), gsd,
     family, datum family, QA (CTX qa_metrics parsed: MOLA diff
     mean/median/std; valid fraction measured from overviews), and asset
     probe status.
T1.3 Deterministic selector. For a window, rank products by
     (family priority HiRISE > CTX > MOLA, QA pass, valid coverage of the
     window, finer gsd, lower |MOLA offset|, newer product id), with stable
     tie-breaks by id. The same inputs always pick the same products.
T1.4 Datum normalisation. All elevations are reported as "metres above MOLA
     areoid".
     For every product, sample at least 200 valid pixels, compare with MOLA,
     and record median, NMAD, offset and the inferred datum family.
     Rules (configurable):
       |median| <= 30 m (HiRISE) or <= 60 m (CTX): accept; record the residual
       offset (do not silently apply it).
       Otherwise, if the offset is about R_sphere - R_areoid (a datum family
       mismatch), convert using the MOLA areoid; else mark DATUM MISMATCH and
       exclude.
     Unit-test with synthetic rasters.
T1.5 Projection and scale. The COGs are Equirectangular with
     standard_parallel = 0, so the east-west ground distance of one pixel is
     res_x * cos(latitude). At Jezero (18.4°N) ignoring this inflates E-W
     distances by about 5% and biases slopes. Implement LocalGrid: either
     reproject the window to "+proj=eqc +lat_ts=<site_lat> +lon_0=<site_lon>
     +R=3396190 +units=m" (bilinear for elevation) or carry per-row
     dx = res_x*cos(lat). Test: a synthetic 10° plane at 18.4°N gives
     10.0 ± 0.1° in every direction.
T1.6 Ladder mosaic: a per-pixel priority mosaic on the LocalGrid with a source
     raster (uint8: 1 HiRISE, 2 CTX, 3 MOLA, 0 none), a product lookup table,
     seam flags and a no-blending rule for analytics (feathering is allowed only
     for display).
T1.7 Endpoints:
     GET /terrain/sources?lat&lon&radius_km
     GET /terrain/hires/window?lat&lon&half_width_m(<=5000)&res_m(>=1)
         &layers=elev,slope_1,slope_5,rough_5,source
         Returns compact JSON (or float32 base64 plus metadata), capped
         at 4 M cells.
     GET /terrain/hires/image?layer=hillshade|ortho|walkability|slope|source
         &bounds...&px...
         Returns a PNG with bounds for a Leaflet ImageOverlay.
     Strict bounds validation (no unbounded reads: half_width <= 5 km,
     res >= 1 m, output <= 2048x2048).
T1.8 evidence.py: move CTX and HiRISE from NOT_INGESTED to collectors (as
     terrain evidence, not science-target evidence).

=== WS3 · Marswalk planner and EVA model (P0) ================================
T3.1 marswalk/config.py. Each parameter is
     {value, unit, kind, citation, note}. Defaults:
       eva_pace_kmh 2.5 (continuity with engine.DEFAULT_EVA_PACE_KMH;
         planning choice)
       ascent_allowance_m_per_h 600 (continuity; planning choice)

       slope_limit_deg 25 at a 3 m baseline (planning choice; matches the
         existing 25° burden reference)

       eva_duration_h options 4 or 8; reserve_fraction 0.3 (planning choice)

     (for example Norcross et al., NASA/TP-2009-214796, incline/gravity
     metabolic tests) [VERIFY]. Where a published value exists, cite it and
     set kind="published"; otherwise keep "planning choice".
     Expose every parameter in the UI with a sensitivity control.
T3.2 Speed model (pick one and document it): Tobler's hiking function
     normalised so v(0°) = eva_pace_kmh:
       v(θ) = pace * exp(-3.5*|tan θ + 0.05|) / exp(-3.5*0.05)
     with θ signed along the direction of travel. Earth-derived; label it
     "Earth heuristic, not validated for suited Mars EVA".
     Keep the Naismith option for continuity with saved routes.
T3.3 Planner: multi-resolution corridor search.
     (a) coarse path on the CTX/MOLA ladder at about 20-50 m;
     (b) buffer the coarse path by 300 m (configurable);
     (c) fine search on the HiRISE ladder at res_m (default 2 m, 1 m allowed
         for under 1.5 km) with skimage.graph.MCP_Geometric or scipy csgraph;
     (d) cells over slope_limit or nodata are impassable; review cells cost
         more;
     (e) where HiRISE ends, the path continues on CTX/MOLA and the segment is
         flagged with its source.
     Deterministic tie-breaking. Return a simplified geometry (RDP at
     res/2) plus the full-resolution profile (capped at 2,000 rows).
T3.4 EVA budget: outbound time plus return time (return along the same path by
     default, or to the nearest declared Safe Haven), with reserve. Compute the
     turnaround point where the elapsed outbound time plus the return time to
     base reaches (1 - reserve) * duration. If the target is beyond the
     turnaround, say so and show how far you can get.
T3.5 Decision summary (no "safe"): counts and lengths of route sections that
     are WITHIN / REVIEW / EXCEED; max slope at 1 m and 3 m baselines; %
     length per terrain source; time budget and margin; walk-back margin;
     science stops reached; dose estimate (from WS6 if present, else
     UNAVAILABLE).
     An overall status is derived only by an explicit rule that is shown in
     the UI.
T3.6 POST /marswalk/evaluate takes any user-drawn polyline (drag-to-edit).
     Same metrics. Under 500 ms warm for 3 km.
T3.7 Reproducibility: a plan id is the hash of request, config, data versions
     and engine version. Extend saved routes to schema v4 with
     {resolution_ladder, data_versions, config_hash}. Write a v3-to-v4
     migration and test it. The existing VERIFY replays the plan and reports
     REPRODUCED or DIFFERS.
AC: on synthetic terrain the planner avoids a wall steeper than the limit and
    stays out of nodata. At Gale, a 1.5 km plan from Curiosity's latest
    position to a fixed test target returns in under 4 s cold (corridor cache
    warm) and under 1.5 s warm. Results are byte-identical across two runs.

APPENDIX A · FORMULAS (implement exactly; document in METHODOLOGY.md)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A1  Ground spacing in an eqc (lat_ts = 0) grid at latitude φ:
      dx_ground = res_x * cos φ ;  dy_ground = res_y
A2  Slope at baseline b (Horn) on DTM Z_b resampled to spacing b:
      p = ((z3+2z6+z9)-(z1+2z4+z7)) / (8 dx) ;
      q = ((z7+2z8+z9)-(z1+2z2+z3)) / (8 dy)
      slope_b = atan(sqrt(p² + q²))   [deg]
A3  Directional slope along travel azimuth α: θ = atan(p sin α + q cos α)
    (signed; uphill positive)
A4  Detrended roughness in a w x w window: RMS(z - plane_fit(z))
A5  Walkability per cell (0-100, higher is easier):
      cost = Σ_i weight_i * min(metric_i / reference_i, 1)
      walk = 100 * (1 - min(cost, 1))
      impassable if slope_3m > slope_limit or nodata
    Weights and references come from marswalk/config.py (default to the
    existing WALKABILITY weights 0.55/0.30/0.15 for slope, roughness and
    relief, rescaled to the metre-scale references).
A6  Speed (Tobler, normalised): v(θ) = pace * exp(-3.5|tanθ + 0.05|) / exp(-0.175)
    Segment time = length / v(θ_segment). The Naismith alternative is kept.
A7  Turnaround: the first point s* where t_out(s) + t_back(s) >= (1 - reserve) * T_EVA
A8  Dose screening: D = r_E * t * A(Δz),  A(Δz) = 1 + k * (exp(-Δz/H) - 1),
    with k (fractional dose change per fractional column change) from V7 or
    the literature, ± its uncertainty. Report D as a median with a band.
A9  Validation stats: bias = mean(d); RMSE; NMAD = 1.4826 * median(|d - median(d)|);
    Spearman ρ; bootstrap 95% CI (1,000 resamples, seed 2026);
    discrete Fréchet and Hausdorff distances in a local metric CRS.
