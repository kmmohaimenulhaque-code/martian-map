0. ROLE AND MISSION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
You are the lead engineer and the scientific-integrity owner of NeuroNexus, a
Mars mission-planning console for the NASA Space Apps 2026 challenge
"Interplanetary Survival Guide: Martian Map".

Challenge statement (verbatim): NASA has explored Mars robotically for decades,
studying its extreme environment, mapping its surface, and collecting many types
of data. Far from home, the first astronauts to set foot on Mars will want the
best map possible, with information on the details of their routes and
destinations, updates on current conditions, and the data needed to complete
their mission quickly and safely. Your challenge is to create a layered,
integrated view of a location or route on the Martian surface that pulls
together data from multiple NASA science missions and could help a human
explorer plan and carry out a successful Marswalk while conducting new and
exciting science along the way.

Your mission in this session has three parts:
- Turn the existing console into a decision instrument that works at the scale
  of a human footstep.
- Prove it against the only ground truth humanity has on Mars: the drives of
  Curiosity and Perseverance.
- Upgrade the console so every layer, heatmap, legend, number and source tag
  is clear, traceable and fast.

Deliver all six outcomes:
  (1) TERRAIN RESOLUTION LADDER. Use HiRISE DTMs (~1 m), then CTX DTMs
      (~20 m), then MOLA (~463 m). Every elevation, slope and route segment
      states which rung of the ladder it came from.
  (2) METER-SCALE MARSWALK PLANNING AND ENERGETICS. Produce a route, an EVA
      time budget, a metabolic and consumables screening estimate, a
      turnaround point (Point of No Return, PNR) and walkability per segment.
      The metabolic estimate is a Pandolf/Norcross-style suited proxy. The
      walking pace comes from Tobler or Naismith.
  (3) CURIOSITY AND PERSEVERANCE TRAVERSES. Show them as map layers, and use
      them as a ground-truth validation dataset.
  (4) REPRODUCIBLE VALIDATION SUITE. Its numbers are generated, versioned and
      shown in the UI and README. No number is typed in by hand.
  (5) TARGETED UI/UX AND PERFORMANCE UPGRADES, in the same dark HUD theme:
      - a categorized layers dropdown and a dynamic map legend
      - a THEMIS thermal heatmap in the left panel
      - a 3D globe upgrade
      - a hamburger menu that becomes a Mission Operations Manual
      - a "Browser Optimization" engine that targets 60 FPS on a 4 GB RAM
        laptop
      - real Playwright screenshots embedded in README.md
  (6) ONE SOURCE LEDGER AND CLEAR SOURCE TAGS (🏷️). There is a single
      canonical, up-to-date source ledger. Every value, layer, chart and
      export in the UI carries a visible, clickable source tag that resolves
      to its ledger entry.

Keep the existing color theme: dark background, cyan glow, and green, amber
and red semantics. Change layout and hierarchy where it serves the decision.
Everything that works today must keep working.

Every layer must help answer one question:
  "Is it within our planning limits to walk here, for this long, today, and
   what science can I do along the way?"

3. THE WINNING THESIS AND THE 90-SECOND DEMO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Thesis (README line 1 and slide 1):
  "Orbital maps say Gale Crater is flat; Curiosity's wheels say otherwise.
   NeuroNexus fuses HiRISE, CTX and MOLA terrain into a meter-scale Marswalk
   planner, validates it against 2,000+ real rover drive end-points, and shows
   the astronaut exactly where the map can be trusted and where it cannot."

Why Gale is the hero site (Jezero is the out-of-sample test):
- A 1 m DEM mosaic covers the whole Curiosity traverse: all 1,386
  end-of-drive waypoints fall within its bounds [VERIFIED 2026-10-20].
- RAD (radiation) and REMS (weather) data come from the same rover at the
  same site.
- About 38.5 km of driving, with rover attitude (tilt, roll, pitch) recorded
  for every drive.
- A climb of about 1 km up Mount Sharp, which is a natural altitude
  experiment.
Jezero runs the same pipeline with no re-tuning. 662 of Perseverance's 706
waypoints fall inside the 1 m Jezero mosaic. The other 44 are on the western
rim and must fall back to HiRISE strips or CTX. Show that fallback honestly.

The 90-second demo (it must also work offline, from the demo pack):
1. Open the hamburger menu (🍔 Mission Operations Manual) and click "Gale
   Crater Marswalk Demo".
   - The map flies from the global MOLA view to a 1 m hillshade plus HiRISE
     orthoimage around Curiosity's latest position.
   - The TERRAIN LADDER chip reads "HiRISE 1.0 m · DTEEC_xxx".
2. Open the CATEGORIZED LAYERS dropdown and the MAP LEGEND.
   - The legend shows only the active keys.
   - Glance at the THEMIS THERMAL HEATMAP in the left panel.
   - Every panel shows 🏷️ source tags. Click one to open its ledger entry.
3. Click a target 1-2 km away and choose a 4-hour EVA. Within about 2 s the
   map shows:
   - a route colored by walkability
   - the turnaround (PNR) marker
   - the time and metabolic/consumables budget
   - red hatching on cells that exceed planning limits
4. Read the ProfileStrip: elevation, slope at 1 m and 5 m baselines, the
   terrain-source ribbon, and cumulative EVA time. Drag a route vertex; the
   update runs off the main thread (Browser Optimization) and stays smooth.
5. Toggle GROUND TRUTH. Curiosity's real drives appear, and the VALIDATION
   card shows the generated numbers, for example:
     "Rover-measured tilt vs our 3 m slope: Spearman ρ = …, n = 1,386.
      MOLA-only slope underestimates the 90th percentile by …×"
6. Switch the terrain source to "MOLA only". The route straightens and the
   hazards disappear. This is the "why meter-scale matters" moment.
7. Read the CONDITIONS strip, each item labeled:
   - the latest archived RAD dose rate (sol N, archive lag D days)
   - the EVA dose band
   - the REMS outreach weather line
   - the modeled dust
8. Export the EVA BRIEF (JSON plus a printable page). Every value in it has
   provenance and a ledger id.

APPENDIX A · FORMULAS (implement these exactly; document them in METHODOLOGY.md)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A1  Ground spacing in an equirectangular grid with lat_ts = 0, at latitude φ:
      dx_ground = res_x * cos φ
      dy_ground = res_y
A2  Slope at baseline b, using Horn's method on the DTM resampled to spacing
    b. With z1..z9 the 3x3 neighborhood (row-major):
      p = ((z3 + 2z6 + z9) - (z1 + 2z4 + z7)) / (8 dx)
      q = ((z7 + 2z8 + z9) - (z1 + 2z2 + z3)) / (8 dy)
      slope_b = atan( sqrt(p² + q²) )                      [deg]
A3  Directional slope along travel azimuth α (signed; uphill is positive):
      θ = atan(p sin α + q cos α)
A4  Detrended roughness over a w x w window:
      roughness = RMS( z - plane_fit(z) )
A5  Walkability per cell, on a 0-100 scale (higher is easier):
      cost = Σ weight_i * min(metric_i / reference_i, 1)
      walk = 100 * (1 - min(cost, 1))
    A cell is impassable if slope_3m > slope_limit or if it is nodata.
A6  Pace (Tobler, normalized):
      v(θ) = pace * exp(-3.5 * |tan θ + 0.05|) / exp(-0.175)
      t_segment = length / v(θ)
    Naismith is kept as an option.
A6b Metabolic proxy (Pandolf 1977 form, gravity-scaled screening).
    Inputs:
      W  body mass (kg)
      L  suit + payload mass (kg)
      V  speed (m/s)
      G  uphill grade (%) = 100 * max(tan θ, 0)
      η  terrain factor
      γ  g_mars / g_earth (about 0.379)
    Rate (W):
      M = 1.5 W + γ * [ 2.0 (W + L)(L / W)² + η (W + L)(1.5 V² + 0.35 V G) ]
    Use the Santee et al. correction for downhill if adopted [VERIFY].
    η comes from Pandolf's published terrain coefficients, mapped from
    roughness. The mapping is a planning choice [VERIFY the coefficients].
    Consumables:
      O2 (kg)    = (M * t / 20.1 kJ per L O2) * 1.429e-3 kg per L
      water (L)  = (f_rej * M * t) / 2.45 MJ per kg
    f_rej (fraction of heat rejected by water) is a planning choice.
    Label: MODELED screening proxy. Suited reduced-gravity cost does not
    scale simply; show a sensitivity band, and compare with the Norcross
    suited data where it is citable.
A7  Point of No Return (PNR): the first s* along the route where
      t_out(s) + t_back(s) >= (1 - reserve) * T_EVA
    Apply the same rule to the O2 budget if a budget is configured.
A8  Dose screening:
      D = r_E * t * A(Δz)
      A(Δz) = 1 + k * (exp(-Δz / H) - 1)
    k comes from V7 or a literature citation, with its uncertainty.
    H = atmospheric scale height [VERIFY the value].
A9  Validation statistics:
      bias = mean(d)
      RMSE
      NMAD = 1.4826 * median(|d - median(d)|)
      Spearman ρ
      bootstrap 95% CI (1,000 resamples, seed 2026)
      discrete Fréchet and Hausdorff distances, computed in a local metric
      CRS
A10 LTTB and RDP decimation are for DISPLAY ONLY. Validation and metrics
    always use full-resolution data.

APPENDIX D · THE 15 QUESTIONS JUDGE_FAQ MUST ANSWER HONESTLY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 1. Where does this slope number come from?
    Product, baseline and method; the 🏷️ tag shows them.
 2. Why should I trust HiRISE here?
    QA, the datum check, and V2.
 3. What can't the 1 m DTM see?
    Rocks, ripples, and regolith strength.
 4. Isn't the rover-vs-HiRISE elevation check circular?
    Yes. V1 checks consistency only; V2 and V3 are the independent checks.
 5. Rovers aren't humans. Why compare routes?
    It tests plausibility, with the caveats stated.
 6. Where do the walking limits come from?
    Each is either a published value or a stated planning choice.
 7. Is the pace or metabolic model valid on Mars?
    It is an Earth/lunar heuristic, shown with a sensitivity band.
 8. Is the radiation number live?
    No. It is archived data with a lag, used as a screening estimate.
 9. Why isn't the route colored by dose?
    At EVA scale the altitude term is under 1%.
10. What happens outside HiRISE coverage?
    The ladder falls back to CTX or MOLA, and the segment is labeled.
11. How do you know the results are reproducible?
    plan_id, recorded data versions, and V5.
12. What did the AI do?
    Interpretation only, through tools. It produces no geometry or numbers.
13. What breaks offline?
    Nothing on the demo path; the demo pack covers it.
14. How does it run on a weak laptop?
    Browser Optimization: a worker, lazy loading, canvas rendering, a
    cache. Measured numbers are in PERFORMANCE.md.
15. Why Gale and Jezero?
    Ground truth is co-located there: 1 m mosaics, rover traverses, and
    RAD/REMS.
