## Overview and success rate
- Many Mars missions have failed (launch failures, cruise losses, landing failures). Landing is the hardest phase: an atmosphere too thin to brake fully on its own but thick enough to cause heating ("seven minutes of terror").
- Landing approaches: Viking (aeroshell, parachute, retro-rockets); airbags (Mars Pathfinder, Spirit, Opportunity); legged powered descent (Phoenix, InSight); Sky Crane (Curiosity, Perseverance).

## Early era (1960–1975)
- Mariner 3 (NASA) failed in 1964 (shroud did not separate).
- Mariner 4 (NASA): first successful Mars flyby, 14–15 July 1965; returned 21 close-up images showing a cratered surface and a thin atmosphere.
- Mariner 6 and 7 (NASA): flybys in July–August 1969.
- Mariner 9 (NASA): first spacecraft to orbit another planet, 14 November 1971. It arrived during a global dust storm, then mapped about 85% of Mars, revealing Olympus Mons and Valles Marineris (named after Mariner).

## Viking (1975–1982)
- Viking 1 lander touched down in Chryse Planitia on 20 July 1976, NASA's first Mars landing and the first long-lived surface mission (operated until November 1982).
- Viking 2 landed in Utopia Planitia on 3 September 1976 (operated until April 1980).
- The two orbiters mapped the planet. The landers' biology experiments returned results now generally attributed to reactive soil chemistry (e.g. perchlorates/oxidants) rather than life.

## 1990–1999
- Mars Observer (NASA): lost in August 1993, days before orbit insertion.
- Mars Global Surveyor (MGS, NASA): orbit September 1997, operated until November 2006. Carried MOLA (laser altimeter — the global topography NeuroNexus uses), TES, MOC camera and magnetometer.
- Mars Pathfinder and Sojourner (NASA): landed 4 July 1997 in Ares Vallis using airbags; Sojourner was the first Mars rover.
- Nozomi (Japan, 1998): failed to enter Mars orbit (2003).
- Mars Climate Orbiter (NASA): lost on 23 September 1999 because of an English/metric unit mismatch in ground software.
- Mars Polar Lander and Deep Space 2 (NASA): lost on 3 December 1999.

## 2001–2011
- 2001 Mars Odyssey (NASA): orbit 24 October 2001. Carries THEMIS (Thermal Emission Imaging System — the source of NeuroNexus historical thermal evidence) and the Gamma Ray Spectrometer, which detected abundant near-surface hydrogen (water ice) at high latitudes. It is the longest-operating spacecraft at Mars; verify its current status.
- Mars Express (ESA): orbit 25 December 2003; HRSC stereo camera, OMEGA spectrometer, MARSIS radar. Its Beagle 2 lander (UK) failed to communicate; HiRISE found it partially deployed on the surface in 2015.
- Spirit (MER-A, NASA): landed 4 January 2004 in Gusev crater; found evidence of hydrothermal activity (silica-rich deposits at Home Plate). Became stuck in 2009; last communication 22 March 2010.
- Opportunity (MER-B, NASA): landed 25 January 2004 in Meridiani Planum; found hematite "blueberries" and sulfate rocks that formed in acidic water; explored Victoria and Endeavour craters. It drove 45.16 km. That set the off-world driving distance record. Its last contact was 10 June 2018 during the MY 34 global dust storm, and the mission was declared complete on 13 February 2019.
- Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (MRO, NASA): orbit 10 March 2006. Carries HiRISE (~25–30 cm/pixel), CTX (~6 m/pixel), CRISM, SHARAD radar and MCS. It is a key communications relay.
- Phoenix (NASA): landed 25 May 2008 at ~68° N in Vastitas Borealis; confirmed near-surface water ice and discovered perchlorate in soil; ended in November 2008 as winter set in.

## 2012–2020
- Curiosity / Mars Science Laboratory (NASA): landed 6 August 2012 (UTC) in Gale crater using the Sky Crane. It is a nuclear-powered (MMRTG) rover climbing Aeolis Mons (Mount Sharp).
  - It established that Gale once held a long-lived, habitable freshwater lake (Yellowknife Bay, 2013).
  - It detected complex organic molecules (2018) and seasonal methane variations.
  - Its RAD instrument measured the surface radiation dose.
- MAVEN (NASA): orbit 21 September 2014; it studies upper-atmosphere loss to space, and showed that solar-wind stripping removed much of the early atmosphere. Verify its current status.
- Mars Orbiter Mission / Mangalyaan (ISRO, India): orbit 24 September 2014, India's first interplanetary mission, successful on its first attempt. Contact was lost in 2022.
- ExoMars Trace Gas Orbiter (ESA): orbit October 2016; studies trace gases (methane upper limits) and mapped hydrogen; also a relay. Its Schiaparelli demonstrator lander crashed on 19 October 2016.
- InSight (NASA): landed 26 November 2018 in Elysium Planitia. Its SEIS seismometer recorded more than 1,300 marsquakes and constrained the size of Mars' crust, mantle and liquid core. The mission ended in December 2022 when dust starved its solar arrays.

## 2021 arrivals
- Hope / Emirates Mars Mission (UAE, built and operated by MBRSC): orbit 9 February 2021; studies global weather and the diurnal cycle from a high orbit.
- Perseverance / Mars 2020 (NASA): landed 18 February 2021 in Jezero crater, an ancient lake with a river delta.
  - It is caching rock cores for possible future return.
  - MOXIE produced 122 g of oxygen from atmospheric CO₂ across 16 runs, the first ISRU demonstration on another planet.
  - The "Cheyava Falls" rock (2024) shows features that are candidate potential biosignatures; these are not confirmed evidence of life.
- Ingenuity Mars Helicopter: the first powered, controlled flight on another planet, on 19 April 2021. It made 72 flights before rotor damage ended its mission in January 2024.

## Planned and proposed missions (verify; plans change)
- JAXA Martian Moons eXploration (MMX): Phobos sample return, planned launch around 2026.
- ESA ExoMars Rosalind Franklin rover: a drill to 2 m depth to search for biosignatures, planned launch around 2028 with NASA contributions.
- Mars Sample Return (NASA–ESA): would retrieve Perseverance's cached samples; its architecture and budget are under review.
- NASA ESCAPADE: twin smallsats to study Mars' magnetosphere and atmospheric escape.
- Human missions: NASA's Moon to Mars architecture targets crewed Mars exploration after Artemis lunar missions; commercial proposals (e.g. SpaceX Starship) exist. No crewed Mars mission is scheduled with a fixed date.

## Missions most relevant to NeuroNexus data
- MGS / MOLA: global topography (elevation, slope, roughness, walkability score).
- Mars Odyssey / THEMIS: historical infrared brightness temperatures (thermal panel).
- MGS TES, Odyssey THEMIS, MRO MCS: retrievals behind the Montabone dust climatology (dust panel).
- Viking: imagery behind USGS mosaics (Viking MDIM 2.1 used in the 3D view).
- Curiosity, Perseverance, Spirit, Opportunity, Sojourner, Phoenix, InSight, Viking 1/2: landing sites and rover traverses shown in the 3D view and the landing-site catalog.
